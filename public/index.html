CASE 01

Asset operations platform for a property group

Real estate and property managementPlatformArabic and EnglishRole-based access

One operations system for a property group's buildings, units, vehicles and stock.

Use case
Operations managers, building supervisors, technicians, procurement and security guards each sign in to their own view of maintenance tickets, approvals, rent collection, vacant-unit inspections, fleet and warehouse.
Problem
Maintenance requests, approvals and rent follow-ups lived in chats and spreadsheets, so late jobs went unnoticed and nobody had one picture of 41 buildings and 471 units.
Solution
A live dashboard that shows jobs waiting for approval, late jobs, fixed-on-time rate, occupancy and rent collected. Every request gets a ticket, an owner and a deadline.
CASE 02

Corporate websites as a service

Banking and listed companiesSaaSBilingualInvestor relations

A ready-to-launch corporate website for Kuwait's banks and listed companies, hosted and supported as a subscription.

Use case
A listed company picks a template and goes live with investor relations, disclosures, governance pages and an Arabic and English version of every page.
Problem
Banks and listed companies must publish disclosures and investor content in two languages, and a custom corporate website can take months to design, build and secure.
Solution
21 ready templates built around the pages investors and regulators look for, with disclosures built in. Hosting, security and support come from KUWAITNET, so a site can go live in a week.
CASE 03

Company intelligence for the Gulf

Data and information servicesData productPortal, API and bulk dataAI connector

Verified legal-entity data for companies in Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain and Oman, from official sources.

Use case
Banks, insurers, procurement and compliance teams verify a company, screen suppliers, investigate owners and clean CRM records before they sign, onboard or pay.
Problem
Gulf registries are mostly Arabic-only and slow, each country uses different identifiers, and data resellers rarely say where a field came from or how fresh it is.
Solution
One search across six registries. Every record names its source and date, is re-checked daily and is marked verified only when two sources agree. Delivered through a portal, a two-way API, bulk files and an AI assistant connector.
CASE 04

Sales portal for account managers and presales

IT services and B2B salesInternal platformCompany sign-inLive reporting

One pipeline for every deal, every department and every presales request.

Use case
Account managers log deals and follow-ups, presales teams receive proposal tickets, and management watches wins on a live office screen.
Problem
Deals, follow-ups and proposal requests were spread across emails, chats and separate trackers, so ownership was unclear and managers had no shared view of the pipeline.
Solution
A single portal with company-only sign-in and admin approval for new users. Pipelines, follow-ups and presales tickets sit together, and closed wins appear live on the office screen.
CASE 05

Online flower and gift store for Kuwait

Retail and e-commerceOnline storeArabic and EnglishWhatsApp ordering

A bilingual online store for bouquets, cakes, balloons and gifts, organised around the way people shop for occasions.

Use case
Customers choose by occasion, such as birthdays, anniversaries, graduation and Eid. They order for themselves or for a company, and can subscribe to regular deliveries.
Problem
Flower buyers shop in a hurry, on their phones and often in Arabic. A generic store makes them hunt through categories and costs sales at peak dates.
Solution
Occasion-led navigation, instant search, a first-order offer, corporate and subscription pages and a WhatsApp button on every screen, all in English and Arabic.
CASE 06

Motion catalogue for an abaya and handbag brand

Fashion and retailCatalogueMobile firstWhatsApp ordering

A catalogue where every piece can be turned, explored and ordered in a few taps.

Use case
Shoppers browse 195 pieces in 8 collections, spin each design to see how it falls, and message the brand on WhatsApp to order or request a custom border or shade.
Problem
A flat product grid does not show how an abaya drapes and moves, and most of the brand's sales happen in conversation, not at a checkout.
Solution
A fast, mobile-first catalogue with drag-to-spin views, collection filters and WhatsApp and Instagram one tap away, so a customer goes from browsing to ordering in one chat.
